## 1. The reference, and what could actually be established

Research route: WebSearch plus WebFetch against StrategyWiki, the Call of Duty
wiki, Giant Bomb and Steam community guides. Three of those four hosts refused
the fetch (403/402), so the mechanical detail below comes from search-result
summaries, not from a page this lane read end to end. That is why the key
bindings row is CLAIMED rather than VERIFIED, and why the implementation
deliberately does not *replace* any existing bind.

| # | Question | Finding | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | What is a drop shot? | Going prone while continuing to fire, so the enemy's aim is dragged down off you. A close-range technique. | **CLAIMED** (consistent across every source found) |
| R2 | Is firing interrupted by the stance change? | No. Sources describe pressing prone *and shooting while going from stance to stance*. Continuous fire is the whole point. | **CLAIMED** |
| R3 | Is there a slide or a dive? | No. The dolphin dive is Black Ops **1**; the slide arrived in later titles. Black Ops 2 had neither, and the owner named both as things he does not want. | **CLAIMED** |
| R4 | Is there an accuracy cost? | Yes — described as a large accuracy loss while dropping, which is exactly why drop shotting is a close-range play. A cost, never a refusal. | **CLAIMED** |
| R5 | How long is the transition? | No source gave a number. Described only as fast, and getting up is the punished half. | **OPEN** — a value was chosen (§2) and documented rather than guessed silently |
| R6 | Default PC prone key | Not establishable. Activision published no default-keybind table for BO2 the way it did for Cold War, and community posts disagree (`Ctrl` for one of crouch/prone, `C` or `Z` for the other). | **OPEN** |
| R7 | Console control | Hold the crouch button to go prone, tap it to crouch — the standard Call of Duty console stance control. Search results confirm BO2 shipped a *Tactical* layout that moves crouch/prone onto the right stick, which only makes sense if the default puts them elsewhere. | **CLAIMED** |

**Consequence for the design.** Because R6 is OPEN, this lane did **not** move
anybody's keys. It kept the existing binds and added the reference's *other*
control, which is a hold rather than a key:

- **Prone (unchanged, remappable):** `Z` or `Left Ctrl` on the keyboard, D-pad
  down on the pad. VERIFIED in `src/key-bindings.ts` and
  `src/input/gamepad/mapping.ts`.
- **Hold crouch to go prone (new, keyboard AND pad):** tap crouch (`C`, pad `B`
  / `Circle`) to crouch as before; hold it past **250 ms** and you drop to
  prone. Releasing does not stand you back up — a drop shot is a commitment,
  not a peek.
- The keybinding panel's Prone row now reads the live crouch bind and says
  *"or hold C"*, so the control is discoverable and follows a rebind.

## 2. Chosen timing, and where it lives

All of it is in `src/prone-transition.ts` → `DROP_SHOT_TIMING`, which is the one
place to tune it. `DROP_SHOT_TIMING_BOUNDS` states the band a future tuning pass
may not leave, and `src/prone-transition.test.ts` enforces it.

| Constant | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `standToProneMs` | 300 | R5 is OPEN; 300 ms reads as "fast but a real fall" and is roughly a third of a second, matching every description found. |
| `proneToStandMs` | 380 | Getting up is the punished half. Pinned by a test to be strictly slower than dropping. |
| `crouchStepMs` | 170 | One stance step is about half the height change. |
| `holdCrouchToProneMs` | 320 | Long enough that a *deliberate* crouch press (~200 ms) never converts, short enough to feel like one motion. It was 250 ms until the review pass: 250 ms is an ordinary press, not a hold. |
| `transitionSpreadPeak` | 1.85 | R4's accuracy cost, peaking at the middle of the fall and back to 1.0 the instant the body is down. **Hip fire only** — `tryFire` applies it to the hip cone and leaves a settled ADS shot the cone it earned. The first version multiplied `computeSpread`'s output unconditionally, which also widened the ADS cone: an undocumented penalty, fixed and source-pinned. |

The hold-to-prone conversion is **armed only when the press came from a
non-prone stance**. `nextStance('prone', 'toggle-crouch')` is `'crouch'`, so a
prone player's crouch press is how they get up; without the arming rule the poll
forced them back down `holdCrouchToProneMs` later and the crouch bind could no
longer stand a prone player up at all. Covered by unit cases and by a browser
case that holds the crouch key from prone for twice the threshold.

The eye follows a **smoothstep**: zero velocity at both ends, and it cannot
overshoot (an overshoot would put the camera below the prone seat, inside the
floor).

`http://127.0.0.1:8188/queue` — GPU 10.5/16.3 GB used). Base `75a4e508`
reproduced by `git checkout 75a4e508 -- src/legacy-main.ts`, restored

| Time from 5% to 95% of that fall | **0 ms** |

The refusal COUNT is frame-rate dependent (the harness calls the fire path once
per frame for a fixed 260/290 ms block): this run says 33, the earlier tracked
run said 30, an independent reviewer's run of the same base said 29. The
property is "the trigger is dead across the whole transition", not a magic
number. An earlier, discarded noisy run quoted 54 and that number reached three
source comments and commit `98f88e4e`'s message; those comments now cite this
receipt, and the commit message is superseded by this section.

Read from source, which explains all of it (VERIFIED):

```ts
// legacy-main.ts, requestStance(), at 75a4e508
cameraHeightOffset = THREE.MathUtils.clamp(cameraHeightOffset + before.y - after.y, -0.12, 0.12);
stanceRecoveryUntil = performance.now() + (target === 'prone' ? 260 : previous === 'prone' ? 290 : 135);
```

The eye moved the entire stance height difference on the press and only 0.12 m
of it was ever smoothed — a teleport with a hint of lag. And `stanceRecoveryUntil`
is consumed by the fire gate (`refuseFire('stance-or-sprint-recovery')`), so the
trigger was dead for 260 ms going down and 290 ms coming up. The shipped game
had the exact two properties the reference does not have.

## 5. The body

### 5.1 Where the body pose comes from (VERIFIED by reading every call site)

`poseOperator` → `updateRiggedOperator` → `applyStancePose` is the one chain
that poses a third-person body. Its call sites, at this head:

| Call site | Stance it passes | Who sees it |
|---|---|---|
| `legacy-main.ts:26369` (stance resolved at `:26355` from `renderedSnapshot.stance`) | the **replicated** stance | a guest looking at another player — the path the ledger's falsifier is about |
| `legacy-main.ts:20179` | `debugBotStanceOverride` | QA only (`setBotPresentation`) |
| `legacy-main.ts:19608`, `:20303` | hardcoded `'stand'` | every gameplay bot |

**Bots never show a prone body, and never did.** `BotPlayer` carries no stance
field at all: the bot AI does not crouch or go prone, so `'stand'` is the honest
pose rather than a drop-shot omission. Both call sites now say so in a comment.
Giving bots a simulated stance is a new behaviour and belongs in its own ledger
row, not in HF-412. The earlier version of this document cited
`legacy-main.ts:20155` as "a bot" call site; that line is a `recordCombatantPose`
lag-comp entry, and the pose call it meant is the QA override at `:20179`.

**Honest finding, unchanged:** the third-person body was *already* blending and
did not snap. The owner's "no animation of the body" impression is far more
likely to be the LOCAL player's camera teleporting (§3) than a remote body
popping. What changed is the SHAPE: the old curve dumps 20% of the pose into the
first frame and is frame-rate coupled; the new one is a smoothstep with zero
velocity at both ends, is frame-rate independent, runs the camera's own window,
and lands exactly on 1. The total 5→95 duration is similar by construction.
Raw: `docs/evidence/pass85/hf412/body-before.json`, `body-after.json`.

**Scope note for Lane Z (rig and clips).** The fixed window applies to
**prone-involved pairs only**. A plain stand↔crouch step keeps the shipped
`1 - exp(-dt * 12)` settle: making every operator in the game crouch more slowly
is not a drop-shot change, was never measured, and lands in Lane Z's file.
Source-pinned in `src/prone-transition.test.ts`.

## 6. No slide, no dive

Stated as code rather than promised in a comment:

- `stanceTransitionImpulse()` returns `{x: 0, y: 0, z: 0}` at every point of
  every transition, and a test asserts it for all four stance pairs.
- A source-pinned test asserts `requestStance` contains no
  `player.velocity.set/add/addScaledVector` and no dive/slide vocabulary.
- The browser test asserts a stationary player who drops travels **< 5 cm**
  horizontally across the whole transition.

## 7. Tests

- `src/prone-transition.test.ts` — **25 tests** (the first version of this
  document said 24; the file had 19, and the review pass added six): the timing band, the transition
  shape (monotonic, no overshoot, no more than a third of the fall per 60 Hz
  frame), the accuracy penalty's presence and bound, the hold-crouch reducer,
  the zero-impulse contract, and **source pins that neither prone arm of the old
  fire block can come back**.
- `tests/e2e/pass85-drop-shot.spec.ts` — headless Chrome, real game: fire
  continuity across the drop, the transition as a fall rather than a frame, the
  runtime's own eye offset matching the smoothstep of its own reported progress
  (frame-rate independent), the bounded spread multiplier, no horizontal travel,
  and tap-crouch vs hold-crouch, plus (added in the review pass) **the crouch
  bind still standing a prone player up** however long it is held. **The first
  two were confirmed to FAIL against `75a4e508`** (`"crouch"` where `"prone"` is
  required; `dropShot` telemetry absent) and **the third fails against this
  lane's own pre-repair head `9096846b`** (the player is forced back prone
  mid-rise), so all three are falsifying, not decorative.
